Website Development Brief

IBM Consulting V2 — Human-Governed Quality Review Microsite

Project Owner: Ibrahem Soliman Yousef Soliman Zahr
Academic Programme: Nexford University — MBA Capstone
Website Type: Professional, responsive, four-page corporate microsite
Purpose: Academic prototype / proposed enterprise service concept

1. What I need you to build

I need a professional corporate microsite that presents a proposed AI-assisted quality review service for enterprise consulting deliverables.

The concept combines:

* Automated checklist gates to identify standard document issues.
* AI-assisted identification and routing of exceptions.
* Human review by qualified managers or senior specialists.
* Final professional approval remains with the human reviewer.

The website should look like a genuine enterprise B2B consulting service website, similar in quality and structure to professional IBM-style corporate websites.

Important: This is an academic prototype developed for an MBA Capstone. It must not look like an officially launched IBM service or claim that IBM has approved or implemented this proposed solution.

2. Website architecture — four independent pages

I need four clearly separated pages with a consistent navigation bar and footer.

Page 1 — Home / Value Proposition

Purpose: Explain the proposed service immediately to a potential enterprise customer.

Main headline:

“Human-Governed Quality Review for Enterprise Consulting Deliverables”

Supporting message:

“Combining automated checklist gates with specialized human review to support more consistent and efficient enterprise consulting deliverable reviews.”

The page should include:

* A professional hero section.
* A short explanation of the service.
* Three or four key value propositions.
* Verified IBM corporate baseline figures.
* A primary CTA button: “Request a Pilot Assessment”.
* A clear visual showing the relationship between AI checks and human approval.

Page 2 — Offering / Service Details

Purpose: Explain exactly what the proposed service does and what the customer receives.

Show the workflow in a clear visual sequence:

1. Customer-approved document submission.
2. Automated checklist screening.
3. Detection and classification of exceptions.
4. Routing flagged items to a qualified human reviewer.
5. Human review, correction and professional approval.
6. Final review report and evaluation summary.

Proposed pilot deliverables:

* Agreed review checklist and acceptance criteria.
* First-pass screening summary.
* Exception register with document references.
* Human reviewer decision log.
* Final sign-off record.
* Pilot evaluation report.

Include a clear section explaining that AI does not make final legal, financial, regulatory or professional approval decisions.

Page 3 — Pricing / Commercial Framing

Purpose: Separate the proposed customer-facing service price from the internal cost of operating the solution.

This page must contain two visually separate areas:

A. Proposed Customer Pricing:

* Pilot package name.
* Proposed price.
* Starting price or price range.
* Pilot scope and included deliverables.
* Conditions and exclusions.

B. Internal Cost of Ownership (TCO):

* Software subscription.
* Implementation and configuration.
* Human supervision.
* Security and governance.
* Integration and data preparation.
* Ongoing maintenance and usage costs.

Do not combine the internal IBM operating costs with the price charged to the customer.

Where figures have not yet been validated, display a clear placeholder instead of inventing a price.

Page 4 — Trust / Governance / CTA

Purpose: Build confidence in the proposed service and explain its boundaries.

Include:

* Human oversight and final approval.
* AI limitations and exception handling.
* Proposed data governance and access controls.
* Privacy and confidentiality considerations.
* Evidence and official source references.
* Academic disclaimer.
* A final CTA.

Add an “Academic Resources” area in the footer containing:

* Research Appendix PDF link.
* Master Excel Model link.

The links will be supplied later.
